3. CAPITAL CURVE -- does more money help?
==============================================================================

  The same machine, the same hours, different account sizes. If returns fall
  as capital rises, the money is coming from your attention rather than from
  your balance -- which changes what you should do next.

       capital     CAGR     profit   EUR/hour  P(loss)    from your HOURS  from your MONEY
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
         2,000   68.47%      1,410       4.71       8%             +1,617              +20
        10,000   20.77%      2,139       6.93       2%             +2,583             +126
        50,000    5.85%      3,011       9.51       2%             +2,583             +547
       200,000    2.96%      6,103      19.91       0%             +2,635           +2,193

  Multiplying the account by 100x multiplied the labour-side
  contribution by 1.6x and the capital-side contribution by 107.3x.

  -> The labour arm does NOT scale with money. It is capped by the hours
     you have, and no amount of capital lifts that ceiling. At small
     account sizes it is nearly all of the profit, which is why the
     percentage return looks impressive and the euro figure does not.
  -> The capital arms DO scale, at a low single-digit percentage. They
     only start to matter in absolute terms above roughly EUR 50,000.
  -> Practical reading: below ~EUR 20,000 this is a tool for spending
     your hours well. Above it, it becomes a tool for allocating money.

5. ABLATION -- is each mechanism actually earning its place?
==============================================================================

  Every component is removed in turn and the system re-run on the SAME
  histories. A mechanism that cannot beat its own absence is decoration.


  arm                                   median   vs baseline   p-value
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
  MONETA (all mechanisms)               20.77%       +0.00pp    1.0000  
  no promotion gate (deploy immediately)    20.98%       +0.05pp    0.9683  
  no Kelly sizing (flat allocations)    20.79%       +0.04pp    0.9721  
  uniform split, no gate, no Kelly      18.95%       -2.14pp    0.0558  
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
  * = difference from the baseline is significant at 5% (Welch's t-test)

  What each arm does to the zero-edge control:
  arm                                        control_null funded   capital
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  full                                                       5%      0.4%
  no promotion gate (deploy immediately)                    10%      0.4%
  no Kelly sizing (flat allocations)                         7%      0.7%
  uniform split, no gate, no Kelly                         100%      7.0%

  HONEST READING OF THIS TABLE. Removing the gate can RAISE the median
  return, and in these runs it does: capital goes to work immediately
  instead of waiting for proof. That is the whole trade. What you buy
  with the gate is the left tail and the guarantee that a strategy with
  no edge at all is funded in 5% of futures instead of 100%.
  MONETA is not claiming the gate makes you more money on average.
  It is claiming it stops you from being confidently wrong with real money.
